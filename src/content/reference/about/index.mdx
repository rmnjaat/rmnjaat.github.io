Everything taught on this site comes from one source: *Use The Index, Luke*, the free web edition of Markus Winand's book *SQL Performance Explained*. This page says who he is, what the book is, and what this site does with it.

## The author

Markus Winand is a database consultant from Vienna, Austria. He started use-the-index-luke.com in 2010 as a free, vendor-independent guide to SQL indexing written for developers rather than database administrators, and he still maintains it. His later site, [modern-sql.com](https://modern-sql.com), covers the SQL language beyond the 1992 standard and how the major databases support it.

He works as a trainer, speaker and consultant through [winand.at](https://winand.at/). The offer is SQL performance and modern SQL training for development teams at companies of any size, on site or as an open online class, plus consulting on specific performance problems. He publishes a newsletter through winand.at/lists and posts on [Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/winand.at), [Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/@MarkusWinand), [LinkedIn](https://at.linkedin.com/in/markuswinand/) and XING; the site has an [RSS feed](https://use-the-index-luke.com/blog/feed) for its blog.

## The book

*SQL Performance Explained* is the printed form of the same material: about 200 pages, available as a paperback and as a PDF from [sql-performance-explained.com](https://sql-performance-explained.com/), and as a paperback on Amazon (ISBN 978-3-9503078-2-5). There is also a French print edition, *SQL : Au cœur des performances*.

The book is for developers. Its premise is that indexing is the one part of database tuning that developers have to own, because the people who know the queries are the only people who can index for them, and it covers everything a developer needs to know about indexes and nothing else: B-tree indexes only, no storage configuration, no hardware. It uses Oracle terminology throughout and gives the equivalent terms and behaviour for MySQL, PostgreSQL and SQL Server in side notes; the web edition later added Db2 (LUW) and SQLite to the execution plan and example script appendices.

The web edition exists in English, German and Spanish in full, and in French and Japanese for most of the chapters. Its example scripts were tested on PostgreSQL 9.0, MySQL 5.1, Oracle 11g Release 2, SQL Server 2008 R2, Db2 (LUW) 9.7 through 11.5, SQLite and Gupta SQLBase 11.7; the execution plan appendix for PostgreSQL states that it applies to PostgreSQL 12 and later. The site also hosts a [three-minute quiz](https://use-the-index-luke.com/3-minute-quiz) with versions for each database; of the first 28,000 people who took it, 40 percent passed.

## What this site is

This is a rewritten edition of that material. It follows the book's structure chapter by chapter and page by page, and it sets out to cover every fact, example, figure and warning the original teaches, so that a reader who finishes a chapter here has met everything in the corresponding chapter there. The text, however, is written from scratch. The original wording is the author's copyright and is not reproduced here; SQL statements, plan output, operation names and the example schema are not prose and may match. Each page links to the page of the original it rewrites, and the [site map](/sql/about/site-map/) lists every page of the original and where its content lives here.

What is added: step-by-step figures of the B-tree, the leaf chain, the join algorithms and paging, a summary at the end of each page, and a SQLite column in the execution plan appendix. Where an engine has changed since the book was written, for example PostgreSQL enabling `BUFFERS` by default or MySQL gaining `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` and hash joins, the reference pages describe the current behaviour and say which version introduced it.

What is left out is everything that is not the book: the blog, which is news and opinion from its time; the shop; the training offers; the legal pages. The site map lists them with links to the originals. If this site is useful to you, the way to support the work it is built on is to [buy the book](https://sql-performance-explained.com/) or to read the [original](https://use-the-index-luke.com/).
